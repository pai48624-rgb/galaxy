 Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs) are a new class of multimodal architectures built for fast inference and on-device deployment. Browse all available models and formats here.
 Copy page Copy page

 All of our models share the following capabilities: 
 32K token context length for extended conversations and document processing (128K for LFM2.5-8B-A1B, 131K for LFM2.5-24B-A2B)
 Designed for fast inference with Transformers , llama.cpp , vLLM , SGLang , MLX , Ollama , and Atomic Chat 
 Trainable via SFT, DPO, VLM, and GRPO workflows with LEAP Finetune , TRL , and Unsloth 
 Start with the model family that matches your input and output shape, then choose a runtime based on where you want to run it. Use the complete matrix below when you need exact repository and format availability. 
 ​ 
 Model Families 
 Text Models
 Chat, tool calling, structured output, and classification. 
 Vision Models
 Image understanding with LFM backbones and custom encoders. 
 Audio Models
 Interleaved audio/text models for TTS, ASR, and voice chat. 
 Decision Models
 Purpose-built models for classification, routing, and scoring. 
 Liquid Nanos
 Task-specific models for extraction, summarization, RAG, and translation. 
 ​ 
 Common Workflows 
 GPU Serving
 Use vLLM or SGLang for high-throughput serving, and Transformers for direct Python inference. 
 Local and On-Device
 Use llama.cpp , Ollama , Atomic Chat , or MLX depending on platform and packaging needs. To embed a model in an iOS, Android, or desktop app, see Build with llama.cpp . 
 Fine-Tuning
 Start with LEAP Finetune for managed workflows, or use TRL and Unsloth for framework-level control. 
 Model Repositories
 Browse LiquidAI collections on Hugging Face for model weights, GGUF exports, MLX packages, ONNX exports, and model cards. 
 ​ 
 Formats 
 Use the format that matches your runtime and deployment target: 
 GGUF — Best for local CPU/GPU inference on any platform. Use with llama.cpp , LM Studio , Ollama , or Atomic Chat . Append -GGUF to any model name.
 MLX — Best for Mac users with Apple Silicon. Leverages unified memory for fast inference via MLX or Atomic Chat . Browse at mlx-community .
 ONNX — Best for production deployment

 Licensing 
 The license, in plain terms.
 Liquid's models are free to use, including commercially. You only pay us once your company's annual revenue passes $10 million.
 Everything is released under the LFM Open License, which grants broad, royalty-free rights to use, modify, and distribute the models. There's no copyleft, so your fine-tunes stay yours, and research, education, and non-profit use are always free with no revenue limit.
 Read the full LFM license 
 Pricing FAQ 
 Common questions
 Is it really free? Yes. Liquid's open foundation models are free to download, run, and fine-tune, including in commercial products, until your company passes $10 million in annual revenue. The full terms live in the LFM Open License . 
 Can I use Liquid in a commercial product? Yes. The license permits commercial use; the only limit is the $10 million annual revenue threshold. Above it, your right to use the models commercially under the free license ends and you'll need a commercial license . 
 If I fine-tune a model, do I have to release it? No. The license has no copyleft requirement, so you can keep your fine-tuned models private and proprietary. 
 What about research, universities, and non-profits? Qualified non-profits, schools, and researchers can use the models for non-commercial and research purposes for free, with no revenue threshold. 
 What happens when we pass $10 million in revenue? Your free commercial rights end and you'll need a commercial license to keep running the models in production. Contact us and we'll help you find the right path. 
 Will Liquid models run on my hardware? LFMs are built to run on CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs, from phones and laptops to vehicles and embedded devices. Model sizes range from a few hundred million to a few billion parameters, with variants small enough to run under 1GB. Browse the model library to match a size to your target hardware. 
 Why run on-device instead of paying per token for a cloud API? On-device inference removes per-token API costs, keeps latency in the millisecond range, and works offline. For sustained or privacy-sensitive workloads, running locally avoids recurring usage bills entirely. You're not metered per request.
