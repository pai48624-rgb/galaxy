Gyeongpo is the best-known beach on Korea's east coast: 1.8 km of sand, 80 m wide, backed by a long belt of pine trees. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the water is about 1 to 2 metres deep with a gentle slope, which is why it is popular with families. Gyeongpodae Pavilion and Gyeongpoho Lake sit about 1.5 km inland, so most visitors pair a swim with a walk or a bike ride around the lake.

It is much quieter than it used to be

Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries figures put Gyeongpo at 5.8 to 6.4 million visitors a year from 2015 to 2018. The count fell from 5.25 million in 2019 to about 300,000 in 2020, and had climbed back to 1.77 million by 2024. That is 34% of the 2019 level. If the beach's reputation makes you expect crowds, the numbers say there is far more room on the sand than there used to be.

Parking: five free public car parks within 550 m

Korea's national parking registry lists five public car parks within 550 metres of the beach. All five are recorded as free and open around the clock, with 709 spaces in total.

Is the water clean?

The Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries tested the seawater at Gyeongpo 12 times during the 2025 swimming season, and all 12 results were rated suitable for swimming. Eight public and open toilets are registered near the beach.

If you need a doctor

The nearest emergency departments are at Gangneung Dongin Hospital (regional emergency centre, 3.3 km) and Gangneung Asan Hospital (major regional emergency centre, 4.9 km). Pharmacies are 2.2 to 3.3 km away, and many close on Sundays. In an emergency call 119. At sea, the Korea Coast Guard number is 122, and the nearest coast guard station, Gangdong, is 1.4 km from the beach.

Where to stay

There are 126 licensed places to stay within 3 km of the beach: 56 serviced residences, 28 hotels, 26 motels and 16 others. Guesthouses and rural pensions are licensed under a different scheme and are not counted here. For campers, the Gyeongpodae Caravan site is 800 m from the beach.
